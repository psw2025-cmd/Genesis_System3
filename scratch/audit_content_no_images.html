Quantitative Options Dashboard: UI/UX Audit

Audited 10 Nov 2026, approximately 19:01-19:06 IST · 1440x900 desktop, 768x1024 tablet, 390x844 phone · read-only inspection

The desktop dashboard is information-rich and its core navigation and chain filters work. The main risks are clipped status information on narrow screens, an over-wide option chain, and readiness labels that combine several different operational states; clarify these before relying on the interface during live operations.

Overall
Strong desktop foundation

Clear dark theme, useful market context, grouped panels, and monospaced financial values.

Highest priority
Responsive and status clarity

Critical header chips disappear off-screen at 390px and 768px; trading readiness wording is easy to misread.

Priority Findings

HighStatus header is clipped on tablet and phone

At 390px, the top header measures 507px wide and the status ribbon 741px; at 768px the ribbon is 1088px. The document itself remains viewport-width, so the overflow is clipped rather than exposed as a clear page-level scroll. Broker/session and alert information at the right edge is partly or wholly out of view.

Recommendation: Reflow or wrap status chips below tablet width. Keep the safety gate, market/feed mode, and connection state visible; move lower-priority ticker items into a deliberate horizontal ticker with an obvious affordance.

HighOption chain is not phone-readable as a full table

The chain has 17 columns. At 390px its table is 1038px wide inside a 382px viewport region, so only a few call-side columns appear initially and the central strike/put-side fields require horizontal scrolling. At desktop, numeric text is about 11.4px. The strike-range filter works, but it only reduces rows, not columns.

Recommendation: Use a phone-specific contract view or let users choose compact column groups. Pin the strike and column headers, add a visible swipe/scroll cue, and retain a desktop full-chain mode.

HighOperational readiness mixes distinct states

The lifecycle banner says ready_for_live=true and proven forward validation. The settled ML checklist also shows “market_validation real run? NO” because state/market_validations is missing, while the shell says PAPER LOCKED / LIVE OFF. Separately, the health panel says WS LIVE while the feed is SESSION REPLAY and live trading is off. These may describe different layers, but the UI does not explain the distinction at a glance.

Recommendation: Display separate, plainly named states for historical model proof, current-session market validation, WebSocket transport, market-data mode/freshness, broker permission, and order gate. Avoid a single green “ready” signal when an independent live-market check is NO.

MediumML proof view flashes an apparent failure before settling

On opening ML Model, the page first showed CHECKING, null metrics, and “Training proof missing.” After roughly five seconds, it loaded one proven performance record (rho 0.78, hit rate 0.75, 204 proof passes) and a populated checklist. The initial screen looks like a data error, not an in-progress request.

Recommendation: Keep a stable loading skeleton until the request completes; distinguish “loading,” “no record,” and “request failed.” Do not display null values as final status.

MediumTwo advanced chart panels show success without visible plots

The Option Chain Heatmap and Put-Call Ratio visibly render charts. IV Surface and Greeks Chart show summary metrics and a green “data loaded successfully” message, but no plot is visible in the captured panels.

Recommendation: Render the plot or replace the success state with a clear “visualization unavailable / no usable data” explanation. A success label should not imply a chart is present when the panel is blank.

MediumNavigation can retain the previous page's scroll offset

After scrolling deeply in Options Intelligence on a phone, selecting Decision Intel opened partway down the new page: the title and lifecycle banner were above the viewport. This makes a successful tab change look like a missing or partial page.

Recommendation: Reset the main content scroller to the top on section changes, or intentionally restore a per-section scroll position and make that behavior predictable.

MediumOne operations card is orphaned on desktop

At 1440px, seven Live Operations cards occupy the first row while “Next open” sits alone on a second row, leaving a large unused area and weakening comparison across the metrics.

Recommendation: Use a deliberate 4-by-2 or 8-column responsive grid, or promote “Next open” into the market-status banner.

What Works Well

  • The dark visual system groups market context, operational metrics, truth/status panels, and model evidence consistently.
  • Core navigation buttons, the mobile navigation drawer, and the option-chain strike selector responded. The selector changed the visible strike rows from 21 to 11 for +/-5 ATM.
  • The page includes a skip-to-content link, named navigation controls, an active-page state, and labels on the option-chain selects.
  • Financial values use monospaced, tabular numerals. Negative moves are red and positive moves are commonly green; signs and text labels also carry meaning, so color is not the only cue.
  • The interface repeatedly states that the inspected session is read-only, market-closed, paper-locked, or live-off. No trade or broker-changing action was taken.

Accessibility and Performance Notes

Numerical typography is consistent, but the option-chain cells are small on desktop and too wide to scan on mobile. Muted labels and status text are much lower emphasis than the bright data colors. A visual check suggests the red/green values are prominent on the dark panels, but no automated WCAG contrast audit was run; verify contrast for all secondary labels and color states, and never rely on red/green alone.

The main observed delay was the ML tab's approximately five-second transition from CHECKING/null to loaded proof. Navigation and the strike filter otherwise responded during inspection. This was not a formal performance profile. The market was closed and the account was in paper/live-off state, so live-feed recovery and order flows could not be verified.

Recommended Fix Order

  1. Make mobile/tablet status and safety information visible, and redesign the chain for narrow screens.
  2. Disambiguate model proof, live-market validation, WebSocket connection, feed mode, and broker/order permission.
  3. Fix loading-state transitions and either render or honestly label unavailable IV/Greeks visualizations.
  4. Reset section scroll on navigation, then polish the Live Operations grid and run a formal WCAG contrast check.

Read-only visual and interaction audit of the supplied dashboard. Viewports tested: 1440x900, 768x1024, and 390x844. The page showed market closed, Dhan paper locked, and live off during inspection. No trade flow was submitted. Screenshot state is time-sensitive; live data and statuses may change.
